We compare neural networks, exact Matérn regression and their combinations on a six-dimensional radiative-transfer table over the 285-band EMIT grid. Across ten random splits, with validation-based selection and 18,884 training samples, input-scaled kernels achieve 0.095% mean relative radiance error, compared with 0.76% for cubic regression and 0.39% for a neural network. Residual correction reduces the network error to 0.14%. A kernel on wider-network features reaches 0.079%, and its convex stack reaches 0.078%. This small forward-error gain accompanies an increase in the mean within-split 95th-percentile reflectance error from 7.57 to 22.71 percentage points, with a larger tail at every split. We derive exact residual-error comparisons and a conditional stability bound relating radiance to reflectance errors. Learning-curve, output-rank and width experiments characterize the effects of representation and tuning. Comparisons with OCO-2 and ten further configurations demonstrate the task dependence of the relative performance of neural and kernel emulators.
