We compare neural networks, exact Matérn regression and their combinations on a six-dimensional radiative-transfer table over the 285-band EMIT grid. Across ten partitions with 18,884 training samples and validation-based selection, an input-scaled kernel gives 0.095% mean relative radiance error, compared with 0.760% for cubic regression and 0.387% for a neural network. Residual correction reduces the network error to 0.141%; a kernel on wider-network features reaches 0.079%. The wide convex stack improves the mean radiance error by only 0.0006 percentage points while increasing the mean within-split 95th-percentile reflectance error from 7.57 to 22.71 points, with a larger tail at every split. For a physically constrained retrieval, we prove a constant-one bound by the transmission-weighted component error divided by the transmission. We give a sharp worst-case transfer exponent under a transmission-tail hypothesis and conditional-error conditions that retain the component-error rate. We also distinguish the raw-error quadratic used to fit retrieval-weighted stacks from the projected, nonlinear retrieval used to score them. The experiments concern interpolation of one adaptively explored table: overlapping partitions and finite-range transmission profiles do not establish independent validation or asymptotic learning rates. Supplementary OCO-2 and multi-fidelity comparisons illustrate how the evaluation metric and input representation change the relative performance of the specified model families.
