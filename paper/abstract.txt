Learned representations and kernel corrections for radiative-transfer emulation

We compare neural networks, exact Matern regression and their combinations on a six-dimensional radiative-transfer table over the 285-band EMIT grid. Over ten partitions with 18,884 training rows, an input-scaled kernel gives 0.095% mean relative radiance error, against 0.760% for cubic regression and 0.387% for a network. Residual correction reduces the network error to 0.141%; a kernel on wider learned features reaches 0.079%. The wide stack gains 0.0006 radiance percentage points on average, but raises the mean within-split 95th-percentile reflectance error from 7.57 to 22.71 points, with a larger reflectance tail at every split. For a physically constrained retrieval, we prove a sharp constant-one bound in component error relative to transmission, construct a predictor attaining the worst-case transfer exponent, and give conditional-error conditions under which no rate is lost. A retained tabulation audit places 0.156% of entries outside the stated physical screen. Its exact-component round-trip RMSE decreases from 7.38e-3 to 1.08e-9 on a reported 99.516%-coverage screen; this is a numerical diagnostic, not a universal accuracy guarantee. A separate one-split refit retains a forward-inverse reversal after screening, but lacks the records needed for a matched reproduction. Training-target sensitivity remains untested. The adaptively explored table and overlapping partitions do not establish independent validation.
