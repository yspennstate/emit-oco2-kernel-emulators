Learned representations and kernel corrections for radiative-transfer emulation

We compare neural networks, exact Matern kernel regression and their combinations as emulators of a six-dimensional radiative-transfer table on the 285-band EMIT grid, and we study how their forward accuracy carries over to reflectance retrieval. Over ten partitions of 23,313 states, a kernel with one length scale per input reaches 0.095% mean relative radiance error and a network 0.387%; a kernel on the concatenated hidden features of five networks reaches 0.076%. Forward and inverse rankings disagree. A convex stack improves on a wide feature kernel by only 0.0006 radiance percentage points, yet its 95th-percentile reflectance error is larger at every partition, three times larger on average. We prove that on the physical domain the constrained retrieval error is at most the retrieval-weighted component error divided by the transmission, with constant one, that the rate this implies is sharp, and that errors proportional to the transmission lose no rate. The reversal persists on the physical domain of the retrieval and when every family is retrained on physically admissible targets only; on a second radiative-transfer code, whose transmissions stay away from zero outside one opaque band, it is much weaker.
