\subsection{Paired comparisons and the radiance--reflectance trade-off}\label{sec:paired-tails}

The saved per-seed records permit a comparison that marginal standard deviations alone do not:
subtract each candidate's error from its reference's error on the same split. Table~\ref{tab:paired}
reports those differences for the main and wide pipelines. Positive gains favor the candidate.
The wide feature kernel improves on the input-scaled kernel at all ten splits, by
$0.0165\pm0.0066$ radiance percentage points. By contrast, the wide stack's improvement over
its own feature kernel is only $0.0006\pm0.0026$ points, with eight wins and two losses.
The latter is a small mean improvement within the repeated-split variability, not compelling
evidence of a practically superior predictor. These are descriptive paired summaries: repeated
splits of one finite table, overlapping test rows and adaptive study development do not supply
ten independent external validation experiments.

Radiance and reflectance also order the heads differently. Table~\ref{tab:tails} reports the
already stored all-band reflectance tails alongside the medians. Each entry averages a
within-split statistic; the mean of ten 95th percentiles is not a pooled 95th percentile.
For the width-512 pipeline, the convex stack lowers radiance error relative to the feature
kernel, but raises the average 95th-percentile reflectance error from $7.75$ to $29.44$
percentage points. The tail is worse at all ten splits, by factors between $2.63$ and $4.94$.
The width-2000 comparison repeats the reversal: the feature kernel has $7.57$ points at the
95th percentile and the stack $22.71$, with the stack worse at all ten splits by factors
between $2.36$ and $3.79$. Thus the near-tie at $0.079\%$ versus $0.078\%$ radiance error
conceals a substantial difference in the inverse metric.

The input-scaled kernel illustrates the same distinction: its median error is only $0.0365$
points but its mean 95th percentile is $31.16$ points. Conversely, the feature kernels have
slightly larger medians and appreciably smaller tails. No head should therefore be called best
without naming the metric, width, candidate pool and spectral domain. The identities of
Section~\ref{sec:stability} explain why a radiance or componentwise convex optimization need not
control the inverse: the predicted denominator must remain separated from zero. They do not
attribute the observed tail difference to any unmeasured component-error correlation.
The result motivates a separately specified retrieval objective and conditioning analysis; it
does not retroactively validate a mask chosen to improve these test scores.

All entries in these two tables are regenerated by
\path{code/make_revision_tables.py} from the twenty individual records, with no model
retraining and no access to raw EMIT arrays. The reanalysis includes checks of the recorded data-file digests,
configuration checks and equality of the paired deterministic baseline rows. These checks
establish consistency of the archived summaries, not independent reproduction of the original
simulator or network training.
