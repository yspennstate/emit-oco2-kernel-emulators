\section{Admissibility, conditioning and the separate retrieval refit}
\label{sec:constrained}

The all-band scores in Table~\ref{tab:tails} combine a change of physical domain,
finite-precision effects and emulator error. These are different issues. This section
retains the reported truth audit and single-split refit, corrects their interpretation,
and states which comparisons the available records do not establish. No clean-target
training experiment is reported here.

\subsection{Physical domain is not the domain of the rational formula}
Write $t=Y_2+Y_3$, $s=Y_4$ and $q=1-\rho s$, with $\rho=0.7$ in this section.
The physical screening rule used in the reported audit is
\[
  \mathcal A=\{t>0,\ 0\le s<1\}.
\]
It is a sufficient domain for the stated physical retrieval analysis, not the largest set
on which the algebraic inverse exists. For example, $s<0$ with $t>0$ gives $q>0$ at
nonnegative reflectance and can leave both rational formulas well-defined, although that
albedo is outside $\mathcal A$. Likewise, $q<0$ violates the physical hypothesis without
being a zero denominator. At $q=0$ the forward expression is undefined. At $t=0$, wherever
the forward expression is defined, $L=Y_1$ is independent of reflectance: the band is
non-identifying. Positive but small $t$ does not itself imply non-identifiability or
impossibility of accurate prediction.

The reported audit covers all $23{,}313\times285=6{,}644{,}205$ entries
(Table~\ref{tab:domain}). It records 438 nonpositive transmissions, with minimum
$-4.5\times10^{-20}$, and 32{,}130 transmissions below $10^{-12}$. There are 9{,}858
negative albedos, with minimum $-0.0724$, and 454 albedos at or above one. Of the latter,
214 equal $2$ exactly. The earlier report identifies these as fill markers; the archive's
generation metadata do not independently document that coding convention. Irrespective
of their origin, they are not albedos in $[0,1)$. At 448 entries $q\le0$, with minimum
$-0.4$. The reported union outside $\mathcal A$ has 10{,}356 entries ($0.156\%$),
occupies 227 bands, and is concentrated near 1841--1908 and 2485--2493\,nm. The audit
reports 18{,}859 states admissible at every band. The category counts overlap and must
not be added to obtain the union. These are retained audit results, not a new raw-array
verification in this revision.

\subsection{A numerical round trip, not a universal accuracy floor}
For comparison with the reported diagnostic, define
\[
 \mathcal C=\mathcal A\cap\{t\ge\tau,\ q\ge\kappa\},
 \qquad \tau=10^{-12},\quad\kappa=0.3.
\]
Here $\tau$ is in the tabulation's flux units. At $\rho=0.7$, the albedo restriction in
$\mathcal A$ already gives $q>0.3$, so the additional $q$ condition is redundant there.
This threshold is a common diagnostic choice in an adaptively explored table, not a
universal floating-point threshold or an independently prespecified deployment rule.
The retained exact-component calculation reports:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
reported round-trip domain & reflectance RMSE & coverage \\
\midrule
all entries & $7.38\times10^{-3}$ & $100\%$ \\
$\mathcal A$ & $4.27\times10^{-3}$ & $99.844\%$ \\
$\mathcal C$ & $1.08\times10^{-9}$ & $99.516\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

These RMSEs are in reflectance units, not percentage points. The last value is an
aggregate error, not a nine-digit guarantee at every entry. The coverage figures refer
to the whole-table calculation and must not be substituted for coverage on a particular
test split. The audit and round-trip numbers are preserved from the earlier report;
their generating sample-level arrays and run manifest are not in the public repository.

In exact arithmetic, substituting exact components in the forward and inverse formulas
recovers $\rho$ whenever the required denominators are nonzero and the band is
identifiable. A nonzero numerical round-trip error is therefore not an emulator error
or a lower bound on what every emulator must incur. In floating point, adding
$\rho t/q$ to $Y_1$ and then subtracting $Y_1$ can lose information. The loss depends
on their relative magnitudes, $\rho$, $q$, precision and rounding, as discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:theory}. The reported reduction on $\mathcal C$ is consistent with
such numerical sensitivity outside that screen. It neither proves that every excluded
entry is numerically unrecoverable nor identifies the cause of every learned predictor's
95th-percentile error. Predicted denominators and the location, sign and dependence of
component errors still matter, as Proposition~1 shows.

\subsection{What the single-split comparison supports}
Table~\ref{tab:constrained} retains a separately reported refit of six families. Its
network radiance error is $0.1741\%$, compared with $0.387\pm0.012\%$ in
Table~\ref{tab:results}. The repository does not contain the refit's matching run JSON,
seed and test-row identifiers, selected hyperparameters, checkpoints or full-precision
prediction arrays. The released campaign driver is therefore not evidence that this
particular refit used the main campaign's complete configuration. The discrepancy is
not attributed to conditioning, a better optimizer or a different split without those
records. This table is not pooled with the ten-split comparison and is not described as
its matched reproduction. Split-specific excluded-entry counts and inversion-failure
counts are also unavailable in the retained aggregate.

Among these six reported rows, the stack has the lowest radiance error, the fourth-lowest
all-band reflectance 95th percentile, and the second-lowest screened 95th percentile.
The feature kernel has higher radiance error but smaller reflectance tails: the
stack-to-feature ratios are $25.67/4.19\simeq6.1$ over all bands and
$15.549/3.117\simeq5.0$ after screening. Thus the same qualitative reversal occurs
inside this separate aggregate after the declared exclusions. That supports a limited
scoring-domain sensitivity observation; it does not establish the conditioned ordering
of the original ten-split predictions.

The restriction does not affect all summaries equally. The displayed median is unchanged
for four families, while cubic ridge changes from $0.480$ to $0.473$ points and the
input-scaled kernel from $0.028$ to $0.027$. The cubic 95th percentile changes only from
$70.68$ to $70.629$ points; the other five decrease by approximately $26$--$39\%$.
These changes describe the retained rows, not a causal decomposition of their errors.

Two features of Figure~\ref{fig:bands} do not appear in the aggregates. The component
residual is smooth in wavelength and widens where the transmitted flux is small, so the
families differ mainly in the amplitude of a shared shape; on the direct flux no family
except the cubic ridge has a band whose 95th-percentile error exceeds half the band's own
scale.

The reflectance residual does not inherit that shape. Over all bands its outer envelopes
open into vertical walls, and those walls have two parts. A common core of 42 bands ---
1357--1447, 1804--1952 and 2441--2493\,nm --- is a wall for every family including the
most accurate, because there the denominator and not the emulator sets the width. Outside
that core each family adds bands in proportion to its own inaccuracy: 42 in total for the
kernel on learned features, 54 for the input-scaled kernel, 66 for the network, 133 for the
cubic ridge, of a 133-band union. Under the conditioning floor the common core is what is
removed, and what remains is closer to what the component errors predict.

\subsection{Training-stage sensitivity remains a separate experiment}
Neither screen changes the targets already used for standardization, PCA, network
training or kernel fitting. The original campaign driver passes the unfiltered targets
to those stages. Consequently, the clean-target effect on the albedo plateau, learned
features and model comparisons is unresolved. A small fraction of invalid entries does
not by itself make that effect negligible, especially when whole spectra are compressed.

The revised driver provides a prospective test using the existing table: retain the
original split, compare unfiltered training with removal of training states that violate
$\mathcal A$ at any band, and include an unfiltered training subset of the same size as
the retained set. Each arm refits all preprocessing on its own training rows; no invalid
value is silently clipped or imputed. Whole-state removal preserves complete spectra for
ordinary PCA but changes the training distribution. The matched-size arm helps separate
data-volume effects from this screening choice; it does not eliminate distributional
confounding or establish that row removal is the unique appropriate repair.

Validation states and the original validation criterion remain common across these
arms, so this is specifically a training-stage sensitivity test, not a fully cleaned
validation protocol. All arms must be scored on the same held-out states and truth-based
screen, with a flux scale derived from the original training block, coverage and failure
counts. A subsequent study can separately assess validation-target screening. The
implemented policies and index bookkeeping are tested synthetically, but no results
from this retraining experiment are claimed here. Existing simulator evaluations are
sufficient inputs; new radiative-transfer simulations are not required for this test.

\subsection{A frozen confirmation on untouched states}
\label{sec:confirm}

Every number above is computed on splits that were inspected while the models were being
chosen. To separate the finding from that inspection, the model list, the hyperparameters,
the admissible-domain constants $\tau$ and $\kappa$, and one hypothesis were fixed in a
pre-registration; a block of $3{,}497$ states was drawn with a seed used in no development
run, held out of every fit, and read once.

The hypothesis fixed in advance was that the family with the lowest radiance error does not
have the lowest all-band $95$th-percentile reflectance error, and that its all-band
$95$th percentile is strictly larger than that of the residual-corrected network.

The reversal reproduces. The convex stack has the lowest radiance error of the six
($0.0687\%$) and the fourth-lowest reflectance tail; the kernel on learned features has a
larger radiance error ($0.0828\%$) and an all-band $95$th percentile smaller by a factor of
$6.60$, and by $5.54$ on the admissible domain. Both factors are slightly larger than the
$6.13$ and $4.99$ measured during development, so the effect is not an artefact of the
splits that were looked at.

The pre-registered hypothesis is nonetheless not supported, and the second of its two
clauses is why. The stack's all-band $95$th percentile is $25.82$ points and the
residual-corrected network's is $26.07$, so the stack's tail is smaller by $0.25$ points
where the hypothesis required it to be strictly larger. The first clause holds and the
ranking reversal it describes is reproduced; the second clause named one particular
comparator and fails on it by a quarter of a percentage point. The distinction worth
drawing is between the qualitative statement, which survives a single read of untouched
data, and the specific ordering among the four intermediate families, which does not
separate at this sample size.
