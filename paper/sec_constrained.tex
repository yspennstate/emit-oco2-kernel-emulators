\section{Retrieval error on the admissible domain}
\label{sec:constrained}

Proposition~1 and the transfer theorem of Section~\ref{sec:theory} describe an entry at
which the transmitted flux is positive and $q = 1 - \rho Y_4$ is bounded away from zero.
The all-band statistics of Table~\ref{tab:tails} include entries at which neither holds,
and there the inverse is not merely ill-conditioned but undefined. This section separates
the two, on a domain fixed before any model is scored.

Admissibility is a property of the tabulation. Over all $23{,}313\times285 = 6{,}644{,}205$
tabulated entries, the transmitted flux $t = Y_2 + Y_3$ is non-positive at 438 entries, with
a minimum of $-4.5\times10^{-20}$, and falls below $10^{-12}$ at 32{,}130. The spherical
albedo lies outside $[0,1)$ at 10{,}312 entries: 9{,}858 are negative, with a minimum of
$-0.0724$, and 454 are at or above one, of which 214 are exactly $2$, a fill marker rather
than an albedo. At 448 entries $q \le 0$, with a minimum of $-0.4$. Writing
$\mathcal{A} = \{t > 0,\ 0 \le Y_4 < 1\}$, the inadmissible set has 10{,}356 entries
($0.156\%$); it occupies 227 of the 285 bands and concentrates in the water-vapour band
between 1841 and 1908\,nm and at the long-wave edge between 2485 and 2493\,nm. Of the
23{,}313 states, 18{,}859 are admissible at every band.

Admissibility alone does not make the inverse stable, because $t$ can be positive and far
below the scale on which $L - Y_1$ is resolved. We therefore fix a conditioning floor in
advance, $\tau = 10^{-12}$ on $t$ and $\kappa = 0.3$ on $q$, and report the retained
coverage beside every number computed on it. On the exact tabulated components the three
regimes separate:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
round trip at exact components & reflectance RMSE & coverage \\
\midrule
all bands & $7.38\times10^{-3}$ & $100\%$ \\
admissible, $t>0$ and $0\le Y_4<1$ & $4.27\times10^{-3}$ & $99.844\%$ \\
with the conditioning floor $t\ge\tau$, $q\ge\kappa$ & $1.08\times10^{-9}$ & $99.516\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The reflectance error of the exact-component round trip is therefore not a property of the
inversion on its domain of definition. It is carried by the $0.48\%$ of entries below the
floor, where the reflectance is unidentifiable or nearly so and a returned inverse measures
cancellation rather than emulator accuracy. A reflectance statistic taken over all bands is
dominated in its tail by that set, and a comparison between emulators conducted over all
bands compares, in its tail, their behaviour where no emulator can be correct.

The restriction is a statement about tails and not about the centre. Across the six
families the median absolute reflectance error is unchanged to three decimals by the
floor, while the 95th percentile falls by a third to a half. Whether a family's tail is
conditioning-dominated is itself informative: the cubic ridge, whose reflectance error is
carried by its own inaccuracy, barely moves ($70.68$ to $70.63$ points), while the network
moves from $37.50$ to $24.11$.

The reversal of Section~\ref{sec:results} is not an artefact of the excluded entries. On
the split reported in Table~\ref{tab:constrained} the convex stack has the lowest radiance
error of the six families and the fifth-lowest reflectance tail; the kernel on learned
features has a larger radiance error and a tail smaller by a factor of $6.1$ over all
bands. Under the conditioning floor that factor is $5.0$. Restricting the evaluation to
the domain on which the retrieval statements hold narrows the gap and leaves it in place.

Two features of Figure~\ref{fig:bands} do not appear in the aggregates. The component
residual is smooth in wavelength and widens where the transmitted flux is small, so the
families differ mainly in the amplitude of a shared shape; on the direct flux no family
except the cubic ridge has a band whose 95th-percentile error exceeds half the band's own
scale.

The reflectance residual does not inherit that shape. Over all bands its outer envelopes
open into vertical walls, and those walls have two parts. A common core of 42 bands ---
1357--1447, 1804--1952 and 2441--2493\,nm --- is a wall for every family including the
most accurate, because there the denominator and not the emulator sets the width. Outside
that core each family adds bands in proportion to its own inaccuracy: 42 in total for the
kernel on learned features, 54 for the input-scaled kernel, 66 for the network, 133 for the
cubic ridge, of a 133-band union. Under the conditioning floor the common core is what is
removed, and what remains is closer to what the component errors predict.
