# Manuscript revision — September 18, 2026

## Scope

This revision starts from scientific commit
`e49a8245eaff63326b325a534a5e372eaa39359d` and preserves its correction that the
three-member wide-feature comparison is exploratory. The manuscript's title,
author, affiliation and funding are retained. The abstract is shortened to
145 words. The body is now framed as an empirical interpolation study with
conditional algebraic analysis, not a proved learning-rate or operational
retrieval result.

## Substantive repairs and additions

1. Replaced the erroneous source-norm “exactly when” comparison by its valid
   upper-bound statement, proved exact squared-error alignment criteria, and
   supplied counterexamples in both directions. Distinguished the direct and
   residual kernels when tuned separately; linked the regularizer to the code's
   `n * nugget`; included the PSD boundary and the actual PCA-projected pipeline.
   The learning-rate discussion now states its missing uniformity and source-norm
   hypotheses rather than treating training loss as their proof.
2. Added finite-error forward-radiance and inverse-reflectance identities and a
   denominator-margin stability bound, with a proof and machine checks. An explicit
   two-band example has vanishing relative radiance error but nonvanishing
   reflectance RMSE, even while all predicted reflectances remain physical. Corrected
   the global bounded-sensitivity assertion for spherical albedo: the 0.49 value
   applies locally at the exact reflectance 0.7, not to arbitrary predictions.
3. Reanalysed twenty original per-seed summaries. Added paired radiance gains,
   ranges and win counts, and full-band reflectance tails. The wide feature
   kernel beats input-scaled KRR on radiance at all ten splits. The wide stack's
   smaller mean radiance error comes with a larger inverse 95th-percentile error
   at all ten splits; small medians do not make it an unconditional winner.
4. Qualified width/optimization comparisons, PCA variance statements, finite-size
   learning slopes, metric interpretations, repeated-table validation and claims
   about retuning. Removed unsupported assertions of universal generalization,
   complete reproducibility or operational throughput.
5. Added a common-mask diagnostic with coverage/failure reporting and explicit
   input precision handling. Changed future prediction exports from float32 to
   float64 to preserve the precision of the original score computation. Original
   result JSON files are not altered or relabelled as new experiments.
6. Updated the table regeneration map and documented missing individual records
   behind some aggregate-only secondary findings. Added the Caponnetto–De Vito
   correction reference; retained the paper's existing source attributions.

## Reproducible checks

Run the commands in `REPRODUCE.md`. The revision tests are synthetic numerical
checks of proved finite identities and diagnostic handling. The table scripts
read archived metrics. They do not run the original neural or kernel training.
`results/revision_20260918/reanalysis.json` records source JSON hashes, paired
statistics, tail comparisons, and the individual-record inventory. Its recorded
raw-array hashes were checked for agreement across records, not independently
verified against raw arrays that are absent here.

## Remaining scope limits

The revision repairs the identified mathematical claims and makes the main
empirical tradeoff auditable. It does not provide absent JPL arrays, simulator
provenance/permissions, missing secondary run artifacts, per-sample double-
precision predictions or an independent untouched evaluation set. The masked
EMIT diagnostic must be run on suitable supplied data before masked error claims
are made. These limitations are stated in the manuscript rather than replaced
with fabricated evidence. Publication remains subject to author review, data
availability requirements and normal independent refereeing.
