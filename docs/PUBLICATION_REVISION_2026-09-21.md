# Publication revision — 21 September 2026

Source manuscript: commit `9184f0e6f910bd51e290747807a525749f28f588`.
This revision preserves the principal ten-split model results and all named mathematical
results. It rewrites the abstract, introduction, discussion, later diagnostic sections,
stacking analysis and availability statements. It does not retrain EMIT, OCO-2 or
multi-fidelity models. The synthetic transfer construction is rerun and archived.

## Mathematical and implementation reconciliation

The earlier prose conflated projection during fitting with projection during retrieval.
`joint_stack.py` actually leaves both flux components unprojected in the fitting loss,
clips the base albedo heads, and fits separate weighted quadratic errors for Y2 and Y3.
The total-flux floor is used after combination in retrieval scoring. Consequently the
fitting objective is convex: it is `J'_tau`, not a projected-inside-loss objective.

The rewritten section derives the factor-four retrieval bound for that raw, separated
surrogate using projection contraction and the four-term square inequality. It keeps the
factor-three total-flux alternative distinct and includes a counterexample explaining why
putting projection inside a squared loss would be a different, potentially nonconvex problem.
Both bounds require the full physical truth domain and a common evaluation distribution.
Neither is a validation-to-test generalization bound.

The historical solver is equality-penalized NNLS followed by weight normalization. Its
mean-relative-norm arm performs six IRLS sweeps. The revised paper no longer calls these
certified exact constrained optimizers. The legacy `inf` threshold is identified as a
separate unweighted-component baseline, not the limit of `J'_tau` or a zero tail probability.
The weighting arithmetic is unchanged. New runs record the objective, solver, projection
order, precision of both validation and test arrays, and complete physical-domain counts.

The sensitivity Jacobian now explicitly holds the kernel and ridge fixed. The RKHS power
function is defined and justified. The finite-class selection result is not attributed to
the reused validation sets. Proof edge cases at zero error and zero reflectance range are
stated, and a missing fixed-query-mean qualification in the perturbation discussion is added.

## Evidence and precision

The transmission table previously pooled seed 101 (three float32 heads, explicit opt-in)
with seeds 104–107 (nine float64 heads). Its comparison now uses only the four common
float64 records, without refitting. These changed means are identified as public-record
reanalysis. The excluded precision experiment remains in the repository and manifest.

The 228 held-out albedo exceedances on seed 104 are entries out of 664,335 state–band
entries, not states out of 2,331. Nonpositive-transmission entries are counted as well.
The legacy violation mask checks only `s <= S`; its zero count and full-table averages
are not presented as a certificate of the theorem's complete hypotheses. Fully admissible
masked averages cannot be reconstructed from the aggregate JSONs alone.

Finite observed transmission slopes do not establish a population tail law or an
asymptotic learning regime. The revised interpretation is error amplification consistent
with the theory, not empirical attainment of its worst-case exponent. The sufficient
proportional-error condition is not described as necessary for rate retention.

The published joint-stacking and concatenated-feature tables are retained historical
aggregates: their generating JSONs are absent. The state-input perturbation rows are also
retained, while feature rows regenerate from eight records. These limitations were not
resolved by inventing provenance or replacing the experiments.

OCO-2 stacks fit row-relative squared losses; their reported errors are mean relative
norms. The nine recorded quadratic KKT gaps range from about 4.17e-6 to 1.52e-5, rather
than all being below 1e-5. The matched-benchmark uniform-average R2 is reconstructed from
archived coefficientwise identities; the generator now fails instead of silently falling
back to variance-weighted R2 when that reconstruction is unavailable.

## Reproduction and presentation

`make_v2_tables.py` writes to the actual paper directory, retains explicitly unavailable
historical tables, and runs under Python 3.11. The record-level inventory is generated by
`revision_manifest.py`. `format_publication_tables.py` separates wide tables into panels
and gives long perturbation, ridge and external-comparison tables multipage layouts.
This removes the original overflowing table pages. Abstract text and LaTeX are synchronized.

The synthetic construction is regenerated with the original seed and two million samples
per beta. Its threshold bounds use an equivalent empirical-CDF calculation to avoid
repeated full-array scans. A regression test checks equivalence to the direct calculation.
The rounded published synthetic table is reproduced and its full JSON is now archived.

The checks comprise the original ten tests plus twelve new stacking, projection,
precision, physical-domain, power-function and synthetic-bound tests. The complete
manuscript is compiled and rendered for inspection. These tests are not a proof of the
scientific validity of unavailable raw data or a substitute for independent validation.

## Still required for full training reproduction

Raw EMIT arrays, prediction archives at their original scoring precision, simulator
version and generation settings, data redistribution permissions, and missing individual
secondary records remain author-supplied requirements. This revision makes their status
explicit; it does not claim that prose changes supply them.
